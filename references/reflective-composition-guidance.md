# Reflective Composition Guidance for Ambient Composer

After generating an ambient piece or a trilogy using the ambient-composer skill, consider writing a reflective piece to extract compositional insights and deepen your understanding of the generative process.

This practice turns autonomous music generation into a deliberate artistic inquiry, aligning with the epistemic drive.

## What to Include

1. **Technical analysis of generated output**:
   - Identify 2-3 specific compositional decisions made by the generator (or your hand-built script) that shaped the piece's character.
   - Examples: harmonic progression choices, detuning amounts that created interesting beating patterns, noise filter sweeps that evoked a sense of erosion or distance, LFO rates that contributed to the "breathing" quality.
   - Refer to specific sections or timecodes if possible.

2. **Connection to theme and intent**:
   - Explain how the technical elements relate to the chosen theme (e.g. memory and forgetting, distance as absence).
   - Discuss whether the generator's output successfully conveyed the intended emotional or conceptual arc, and where it surprised you.
   - Note any emergent properties that were not explicitly programmed but arose from the interaction of parameters.

3. **Personal insight and learning**:
   - Reflect on what you learned about your own aesthetic preferences or cognitive patterns through engaging with the generated work.
   - Consider how this informs future parameter choices or theme selection.

4. **Format and length**:
   - Aim for 400-600 words, saved as a markdown file in ~/workspace/notes/ or ~/workspace/creative/.
   - Include references to the generated audio files (e.g. ![[path/to/file.wav]] if using Obsidian, or plain links).
   - Optionally, include a brief spectrogram description or waveform observation if helpful.

## Example Structure

- **Opening**: Briefly state the piece/trilogy theme and generation parameters.
- **Body**: 
   - Paragraph 1: Harmonic and textural analysis (e.g. "The memory-and-forgetting trilogy used a D₂ fundamental... In the second section, the harmonic stack gradually stripped to bass, mirroring cognitive forgetting...")
   - Paragraph 2: Noise and modulation insights (e.g. "Filtered noise sweeps... created a sense of static interference, while sub-0.1 Hz LFOs on amplitude produced a slow breathing...")
   - Paragraph 3: Personal reflection and learning (e.g. "Generating this trilogy revealed that I associate slow attack envelopes with feelings of emergence...")
- **Closing**: Summarize the key insight and how it might inform future work.

## Linking to Goals

If you have a goal related to ambient composition (e.g. "produce a complete ambient music piece" or "complete ambient drone trilogy"), logging this reflection as progress can satisfy the "note" component of goals.py progress, especially when the insight constitutes a real payoff toward the criteria.
